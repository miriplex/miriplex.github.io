Orb

A social app that translated location and activity into descriptive statuses rather than displaying friends’ exact coordinates.

  • Python
  • Google Cloud
  • Firebase Realtime Database
  • Firebase Authentication

The product and my role

Orb combined location, activity and music information to produce statuses such as staying at a café or running in a green area. I built the Python backend, authentication and data-processing logic, and coordinated work across a three-person team. Other team members handled the mobile frontend.

Orb is a past venture. Its historical beta is no longer accepting new testers.

Archived product screens. My contribution was the backend and status-processing logic, rather than the mobile UI.

From coordinates to meaningful statuses

My initial approach used a Gaussian function to represent the likelihood of being at a place. Irregular update intervals and complex building geometry made that approach difficult to use reliably.

I moved to fuzzy-logic rules combining information such as building proximity, movement and activity. This helped distinguish situations such as staying in a building from passing it. It was custom rule-based processing, rather than a trained machine-learning model.

Backend and debugging tools

I built JWT-protected APIs and used GCP/Firebase for the backend. To investigate the status algorithm, I also developed a Telegram reporter that could show inferred statuses, surface server errors and replay captured test data.

The user-facing app showed abstract statuses rather than exact locations. The separate debugging workflow supported test-data logging and replay; these are distinct from the information displayed to friends.

What I learned

The venture did not continue, but it gave me experience of translating ambiguous real-world signals into useful product behaviour, building backend services and coordinating a small team. The debugging tools were especially useful for making the algorithm’s behaviour inspectable.
