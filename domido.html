Automating parts of development

I designed and regularly use a multi-agent Codex pipeline at DomiDO. I contribute to detailed specifications and use the workflow to investigate gaps, refine the approach and implement features against the agreed requirements.

  1. 01Specification & my design input
  2. 02Coordinated investigation, planning & implementation
  3. 03Independent critique & review
  4. 04Evidence-based judgement & revisions

A coordinator delegates investigation, planning and implementation. Reviewers challenge the proposed approach and completed work. A separate judge assesses the findings against requirements and test evidence, helping determine what needs revision.

The process keeps my design input and review central. I do not treat an agent’s claim of completion as a substitute for checking the result.

Application delivery through CI/CD

I built GitLab CI/CD pipelines to automate tests, builds, previews and deployments. The frontend and Go backend have independent pipelines that publish container images; a separate infrastructure repository manages deployment to K3s.

Frontend pipelineTests → build / preview → image
Backend pipelineTests → build → image

Both feed a separate infrastructure repository → K3s deployment

This separation makes the boundary between building an application and deploying its infrastructure explicit.

My role

Alongside the workflows, I work on Go backend APIs, validation rules and interfaces for product features. My contribution combines design input, workflow configuration and review of the resulting implementation.
